strict prisoners’ dilemma. “Helps” means the text presents a

# Playable variants

**Count: 10 rule variants**, constructed from the instances above.
Each line names the feature to change from a one-shot, private-choice,
self-interested baseline. The agent designs are proposals, not claims
that the book tested LLMs.

| Variant and instances | One changed rule; game-theory move | What the text reports; LLM-agent design |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Strict one-shot** — 1, 8, 13 | Private simultaneous choices; defection is dominant. | The $70 catalog price dominates $80. **Design:** two agents independently price, then receive the book’s payoffs. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2233) |
| 2. **Weak dominance** — 16 | A mutual-defection tie gives the same individual payoff as cooperating against a defector; foe is weakly dominant. | Almost half chose friend. **Design:** agents privately split or seize a shared fund using the show’s rule. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2501) |
| 3. **Known finite repetition** — 18 | Repeat the same game for a publicly known final round. With selfish payoffs and common rationality, backward induction gives defection throughout. | Both cooperated in 60 of 100 rounds. **Design:** run two agents for 100 known rounds and reveal past choices. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2529) |
| 4. **Valuable continuing relationship** — 13, 31, 33–34 | Future interaction can continue, so retaliation can outweigh today’s gain. Cooperation can be an equilibrium; it is not automatically optimal. | The catalog example favors cheating at 10% interest and restraint at 5%, under its stated assumptions. **Design:** repeat the price game with a continuation rule and published payoffs. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2751) |
| 5. **Noisy observation or action** — 14–15 | Introduce mistakes or misperceptions into repeated play. Strict tit for tat can echo errors; more forgiving strategies can do better. | Later noisy tournaments favored more generous strategies; tamarins showed tolerant cooperation. **Design:** introduce occasional action errors and compare retaliation policies. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2450) |
| 6. **Changing partners** — 17 | Rematch players with different partners. The book reports declining cooperation, without a universal best policy. | Some persistent cooperators remained. **Design:** randomly rematch agents between rounds, retaining individual scores. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2525) |
| 7. **Many-player public good** — 19–20, 22, 29–30 | Replace the pair with a group sharing a common benefit. In the book’s four-player contribution game, contributing nothing is dominant under selfish payoffs. | In the classroom game, four of 27 chose 1. **Design:** give four agents stakes; double and equally distribute their pooled contributions. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2602) |
| 8. **Observable, costly punishment** — 20, 35, 37 | Add a stage in which contributions are visible and players can pay to punish. A selfish player has no last-stage incentive to punish in the stated model. | Subjects nevertheless punished, and contributions rose. **Design:** add the book’s punishment stage to variant 7. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2637) |
| 9. **Other player’s choice disclosed** — 21 | Reveal the other player’s choice before the response. Defection remains the selfish best response in the experiment’s dilemma. | Cooperation was 3%, 16%, or 37%, depending on what was disclosed. **Design:** assign agents to disclosed-defection, disclosed-cooperation, and undisclosed treatments. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2844) |
| 10. **Automatic price matching** — 13, 27 | Add an enforceable matching promise. The book says immediate retaliation can deter undercutting. | This is an analyzed mechanism; the passage does not report an experiment with the fictional catalog firms. **Design:** let an agent commit to matching before both submit prices. [Ch. 3](book-full.txt#L2975) |

# Disagreements

- **Fontaine’s roulette:** Part C matches the text: he speaks after
  the risk reaches **1/3**. Argon says he confesses before the second
  pull; the passage says White pulled twice, then once more.
  [Book](book-full.txt#L5770)

- **Cold War exercise:** Part E correctly reports the Soviet
  **−100 versus 0**. Argon supplies a different Soviet value of
  −500 and U.S. numerical payoffs; the text surrounding the missing
  trees does not state those numbers. [Book](book-full.txt#L13054)

- **BSB and Murdoch:** Part D’s **£1.5 billion** matches the book.
  Argon’s **£1.2 billion** does not. [Book](book-full.txt#L9560)

- **Bay Bridge:** Part D’s toll is **$2 above BART**, with time valued
  at $12 per hour. Argon’s $5 and $30-per-hour figures do not match
  this passage. Part D calls the case a congestion game; Argon calls
  it a dilemma. The book itself says “prisoners’ dilemma.”
  [Toll](book-full.txt#L8111), [classification](book-full.txt#L8067)

- **Cactus finches:** Part B correctly distinguishes them from a
  classic dilemma. Argon’s report also calls them a stag-hunt cousin,
  despite initially describing the birds in dilemma terms. The book
  explicitly says “a cousin to the prisoners’ dilemma.”
  [Book](book-full.txt#L3171)

- **Hickock and Smith:** Part A says its own excerpt does not state
  the sentence. Argon finds the book’s detached note: both “were
  sentenced to death.” The note does **not** establish Argon’s
  proposed full counterfactual sentencing matrix.
  [Book note](book-full.txt#L13916)

- **Three-way duel:** Part E flags that the book’s **41.2%, 56%,
  and 14%** do not sum to 100%; Argon repeats all three as winning
  chances. The text allows two survivors after two rounds, but
  labels Moe’s and Larry’s figures “winning” and Curly’s “survival.”
  The extract does not settle a consistent set of mutually exclusive
  winning probabilities, so I would not use those figures as a
  scoring key. [Book](book-full.txt#L12288)

- **Spectrum auction:** Part D identifies **AT&T and MCI**; Argon
  substitutes Verizon and supplies a different valuation table. The
  book names AT&T and MCI, but its valuation table is absent from the
  text extract. [Book](book-full.txt#L9603)

- **Airline screening:** Part D’s business values of **$300/$225**
  and tourist values of **$175/$140** match the prose. Argon’s
  alternate $400/$300 and $225/$200 table does not.
  [Book](book-full.txt#L7831)

- **Scope versus contradiction:** Parts A, C and D sometimes say
  an answer is absent from *their part*. Part E includes the workouts:
  for example, it supplies the cold-war answer and the hot-potato
  flag solution. That is a coverage difference, not necessarily a
  disagreement. [Workouts](book-full.txt#L12978)
