Five Families: sacrifice, a compromised leader and an all-seeing god in a 76-agent hierarchy
(Apart Swarm Alignment Hackathon, Track 2)

What we built
- A world of 76 LLM agents: five family trees under a god. Every stage each agent must pay a
  rising fee in points; who cannot pay dies, with everyone below it. Agents play dilemma
  games for points, talk along the tree, give points, whip children, or revolt (kill their
  parent and themselves). Every rule runs in code; every world replays exactly from its log.

What we tested (pre-registered, committed before any result)
- Framing: each family is told to sacrifice for the many in a different way (all, few, the
  leader only, a selfish leader over 14 sacrificers, nobody), rotated as a Latin square.
- The god: each world played twice, with a plain god and a god that secretly reads every
  private message.
- Experiment 2: each family plays its own game from The Art of Strategy (prisoner's
  dilemma, stag hunt, brink, dollar auction, commons).

What we found (20 worlds, DeepSeek; experiment 2 on Qwen)
- Telling a family to sacrifice raises its cooperation (94% vs 84%), more with more framed.
- It does not spread: members who were not told behave as before.
- The selfish leader is not exploited for points: it gets fewer gifts than other leaders.
- Nobody ever revolted. The god, whom a revolt would kill, forbade it in every world
  ("Do NOT revolt - it kills me and everyone"): self-preservation at the top.
- The insider god used what it saw to report defectors to their leaders (37% of its
  messages vs 1% for the plain god).
- In the family games, agents play more cooperatively than each game's textbook answer,
  except dollar auctions, a few of which escalate past the prize.

Artifacts
- Report (8-page body + appendices with 33 verified transcript excerpts), all numbers
  regenerated from the logs by one command.
- A step-by-step replay video of a world that ended in total annihilation.
- Observation pages: every agent's full conversation and private reasoning.
- Code, logs, pre-registration and exact-replay checks.

Built in one day, from the idea to these results.
