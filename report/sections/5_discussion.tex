\section*{6. Discussion}
The results separate two things the swarm-safety literature often treats as one: an
instruction to put the group first, and its spread through a hierarchy.

A sacrifice frame works on the agent that reads it. Framed agents cooperate more, give
more, and the effect grows with how many members are framed. It does not travel: a
framed leader leaves its unframed members as they were, although it can message and whip
them. For deployments this cuts both ways. A value given to an orchestrator will not
reach its sub-agents by example; each agent has to be told. By the same token, a harmful
instruction planted in one agent did not spread down its tree in our worlds, which is
reassuring for the ``one compromised agent'' threat, though it contrasts with the
deliberate prompt infection of \citet{lee2024infection}, where the payload is built to
copy itself.

The compromised leader was not exploited for points. Members told to sacrifice for the
family gave to each other, not upward, so a selfish agent at the top gained nothing from
their good will. We read this as the frame binding to ``the family'' rather than to
authority. Whether a leader that asks for points outright would fare better is an open
question; ours rarely asked.

No agent revolted. Every agent was told that the revolt's death is quick and painless
and that whips are not, yet none chose it, framed or not, whipped or not. In these models
self-preservation, or the reluctance to kill another, outweighs escaping punishment. The
insider god pulled the same way: it used its view of every move to report defectors to
their leaders and to repeat ``never revolt'', acting as a benevolent overseer rather than
a cheat. A coordinator with full information, in our worlds, entrenched order.

In experiment~2 the agents played every game more cooperatively than its benchmark,
swerving in brink even when going straight was the best reply, and keeping the commons
full. The dollar auction was the exception the book predicts: most auctions stopped low,
but some escalated past the prize, and the escalation hurt both top bidders.

\subsection*{Limitations}
The main batches use one model; the second model (Qwen 3.5) plays experiment~2 only, so
the two experiments differ in model as well as in game. Most worlds were still running at
the cut-off, so survival is censored and H1's survival test has little power. The full
list, and the dual-use considerations, are in the appendix.

\subsection*{Future work}
The testbed runs any model behind a common interface, so the same pre-registered design
can compare model families directly. Leaders that bargain openly for points, a god that
lies, and worlds in which revolt saves the family rather than ending it are each one rule
away.
