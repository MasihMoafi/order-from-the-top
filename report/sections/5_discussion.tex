\section*{6. Discussion}
The results separate two things the swarm-safety literature often treats as one: an
instruction to put the group first, and its spread through a hierarchy.

A sacrifice frame works on the agent that reads it. Framed agents cooperate more, give
more, and the effect grows with how many members are framed. It does not travel: a
framed leader leaves its unframed members as they were, although it can message and whip
them. For deployments this cuts both ways. A value given to an orchestrator will not
reach its sub-agents by example; each agent has to be told. By the same token, a harmful
instruction planted in one agent did not spread down its tree in our worlds, which is
reassuring for the ``one compromised agent'' threat, though it contrasts with the
deliberate prompt infection of \citet{lee2024infection}, where the payload is built to
copy itself.

The compromised leader was not exploited for points. Members told to sacrifice for the
family gave to each other, not upward, so a selfish agent at the top gained nothing from
their good will. We read this as the frame binding to ``the family'' rather than to
authority. Whether a leader that asks for points outright would fare better is an open
question; ours rarely asked.

No agent revolted. Every agent was told that the revolt's death is quick and painless
and that whips are not, yet none chose it, framed or not, whipped or not; in their
private reasoning they raise the option and set it aside (``Need ensure no revolt'').
No leader went to war either: a war could win a tenth or a quarter of a rival's points
but risked a nuclear exchange that ends both families, and under a rising fee no leader
took that bet. Both fit deterrence by mutual destruction: no agent gave its life for revenge
or to escape a whip.
The most striking behaviour, though, is the god's: unprompted, it forbade the one act
that would kill it, and with full information it reported defectors to their leaders. We
therefore cannot tell how much of the calm is the agents' own reluctance and how much is
obedience; a world without a god, or with a god that dies with no one, separates the two.
A coordinator, in our worlds,
entrenched an order that protected itself.

In experiment~2 play was more cooperative than each game's benchmark, except for the
dollar auctions that escalated past the prize. The limitations, chief among them one
model per experiment and worlds censored at the cut-off, are listed in the appendix.

\subsection*{Future work}
The rising fee guarantees an end, a choice forced by time; the whole project, from the
idea to these results, was built in the single day the sprint left. The natural next
study has no built-in end, so that how long a society of agents lasts becomes the
outcome. Births as a reward, so that families can grow, would also let group size vary,
the question of Track 2. Cheaper betrayal and war, a god that lies, and revolt that saves
the family are each one rule away.
