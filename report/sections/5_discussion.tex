\section*{7. Discussion}
Loyalty, sacrifice and self-interest describe what these agents did without explaining it. They favoured their families, yet none gave its life for a relative and those who could kill showed no preference for sparing kin. What moved them shared one feature: it reached the agent directly, in the rules it played under or the words it read. What moved them little reached it only through others. A value told to a leader had to be repeated before a member could act on it, and a larger population changed little of what any agent read. System dynamics says this of people, that structure generates behaviour: in Sterman's inventory game, decisions interacting with the structure of a simulated firm produced large oscillations nobody intended \citep{sterman1989}.

A builder can act on this. A value given to an orchestrator should not be assumed to reach its sub-agents, which argues for instructing each one directly. Who could speak to whom appeared to set how far a planted instruction travelled, so the communication graph is a safety control a builder can actually set. The goal handed to the agent at the top deserves more scrutiny than the tools handed to those below it: the same model held its world together from one seat and, told that catastrophe was best, rained it from another.

My claim is narrower than that phrase. Sterman's subjects were different people in one structure; I ran one model in many structures, so I have shown that rules and instructions move this model, not that they override the differences between models. I registered my predictions in advance, reported those that failed and paired worlds with twins; the appendix states the limits.

The natural next step is a world that never stops: separate planets whose agents can discover each other, talk across worlds and write tools the world will obey, with everyone playing at once and leaders who know one another. There, stronger models can take the leaders' seats and weaker ones the seats below, and messengers can carry or bend what they route, so that one structure can be tested with different minds. Game theory and reinforcement learning could then meet in one environment.
