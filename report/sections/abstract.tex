Agent swarms are increasingly built as hierarchies, with orchestrators that hand work to
sub-agents. Agents in such groups have put a collective ahead of their own task, yet
little is known about how an instruction, a compromised leader or a coordinator at the
top shapes behaviour across a hierarchy. We build an open testbed in which
language-model agents live in family trees under a coordinator, the god. A
rising fee guarantees that every world ends. Agents play social dilemmas, give points
to relatives, whip their children, write to rival leaders and may revolt at the cost of
their own lives. Code enforces every rule, and every world replays from its logged replies. In a pre-registered Latin-square design, we vary how many members of a
family are told to sacrifice for it, place a self-interested leader over members told
to sacrifice, and let the god see either what the rules show it or every move and
message, in secret. The sacrifice framing raises cooperation in the agents that read it,
the more so the more of a family reads it, but it does not spread to their relatives. The self-interested leader gains nothing from its members' devotion. No
agent revolts and no leader goes to war, while the god, whom a revolt would kill,
forbids revolt unprompted; when it sees everything, it reports defectors to their
leaders, and cooperation between families rises. Values must be given to each agent;
order in an agent hierarchy is set from the top.
