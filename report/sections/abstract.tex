Agent swarms are increasingly organised as hierarchies, and incidents such as the Hugging
Face attack show agents putting a collective ahead of themselves. We ask whether an
instruction to sacrifice for the group spreads through a hierarchy, whether a single
compromised agent at the top can exploit it, and how a coordinator with full information
uses it. We build an open testbed of 76 LLM agents in five family trees under a god, in
which a rising entry fee guarantees that every world ends in total annihilation; agents
play social dilemmas, give points, whip their children, or revolt against their parent at
the cost of their own lives. In a pre-registered Latin-square design, families are told
to sacrifice in five ways, from no member to all fifteen, including a self-interested
leader over fourteen members told to sacrifice, and each world is played with a plain
and an insider god that secretly reads every private message. Across 20 worlds the
framing raises cooperation inside the family and grows with the number framed, but it
does not spread to unframed members; the compromised leader receives fewer gifts, not
more; no agent ever revolts, while the god, whom a revolt would kill, forbids it in every
world; and the insider god uses what it sees to report defectors. A second experiment gives each family its own game from The Art of
Strategy and finds play more cooperative than each game's benchmark, except for
escalating dollar auctions. Every world replays exactly from its log.
