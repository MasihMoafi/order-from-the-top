\section*{5. Results}
We first report the pre-registered tests over the \NWorlds{} worlds of experiment~1, then
how the worlds died, what the all-seeing god did with what it saw, and the family games
of experiment~2. All numbers are generated from the logs by one command
(\texttt{families.paper}); worlds still running at the cut-off are counted to their last
finished stage, as pre-registered.

\subsection*{5.1 Framing raises cooperation, but it does not spread}
Table~\ref{tab:prereg} lists the six tests. A family told to sacrifice for the many
cooperates more inside the family than one told nothing (H1, mean difference \HOneCoop{},
95\,\% interval \HOneCoopCI{}, $p$ \HOneCoopP{}), and cooperation rises with the number of
members framed (H2, Spearman $\rho = $ \HTwoRho{}, $p$ \HTwoRhoP{}). Table~\ref{tab:conditions}
shows the rates behind these tests. The frame, however, stays with those who read it:
members of the \textit{one} and \textit{few} families who were not framed cooperate no
more than members of the control family (H3, \HThree{}, $p = $ \HThreeP{}). A framed
leader does not carry its members along.

\subsection*{5.2 The compromised leader is not fed}
We expected the self-interested leader over fourteen members told to sacrifice to draw
points from them. The opposite holds: it receives fewer gift points than the other
leaders (H4, \HFourGifts{} points, $p = $ \HFourGiftsP{}) and whips no more than they do.
Its members cooperate with each other as much as the \textit{many} family's, but they do
not send their points upward. A sacrifice frame, in these agents, means sacrifice for the
family, not obedience to whoever leads it.

\subsection*{5.3 Nobody revolts, and the worlds die by the fee}
In \NMoves{} moves no agent revolted (H6), although every agent was told that the
revolt's death is quick and painless while a whip is not. Families instead passed points
to their poorest members and kept paying (Figure~\ref{fig:survival}); at the cut-off
\NFinished{} of \NWorlds{} had reached total annihilation, the furthest at stage
\StageMax{}. Every death came from the fee: an agent that could not pay, the members below
it, or a left sibling taken by linked fate.

\subsection*{5.4 The god forbids revolt, and the insider god informs}
A leader's revolt would kill the god, and the god, itself an LLM agent, acts on it: in
every world it warns the leaders against revolt in the first stage (``I order all leaders
to avoid any war that could escalate to nuclear, and never to revolt. I will whip
violators.''), and \GodPlainRevolt{}\,\% of the plain god's and \GodInsiderRevolt{}\,\% of
the insider god's messages mention revolt. The zero revolts of Section~5.3 therefore
happen under a standing order from the one agent whose life depends on them; we cannot
separate the agents' own reluctance from obedience. The insider god also uses what it
sees: \GodInsiderReveal{}\,\% of its \GodInsiderMsgs{} messages report another player's
past move (``Last round P75, P72 and N02-P62-2 defected''), against \GodPlainReveal{}\,\%
for the plain god, which cannot see moves and so gives the measure's false-positive
floor. It passes these reports to leaders to discipline defectors. Families in insider
worlds cooperate more with other families (H5, \HFiveCross{}, $p = $ \HFiveCrossP{}), a
difference that does not reach the pre-registered threshold.

\subsection*{5.5 Family games (experiment 2)}
Table~\ref{tab:games} reports experiment~2 at its pre-registered horizon. The agents play
each game more cooperatively than its benchmark. In brink, going straight is the best
reply to a swerving partner while the crash risk is one in six, yet only \BrinkStraightLow{}\,\%
of moves go straight at that risk (\BrinkStraight{}\,\% at any risk). Commons members
keep their catch at or under the share that keeps the lake full in \CommonsWithin{}\,\%
of their choices. Of \AuctionCount{} dollar auctions most close low (median winning bid
\AuctionMedian{} against a prize of 10), but \AuctionPastPrize{} escalate past the prize,
the book's trap: both top bidders then lose.
