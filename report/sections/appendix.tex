\section*{Appendix: Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations}

\subsection*{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One model per experiment.} Experiment~1 uses \texttt{deepseek-v4-flash}
  and experiment~2 uses \texttt{qwen3.5-397b}, so the experiments differ in model as well
  as in game, and no claim is made across models.
  \item \textbf{A contradictory mafia instruction.} In experiment~1 the prompt said to
  use the mafia option only when an arrest is shown, while the rule checker required it
  whenever a mafia partner is shown. Replies that followed the prompt were rejected and
  their rounds aborted (one stage of b7-insider-s1 was replayed more than ten times).
  Experiment~2 uses the corrected instruction.
  \item \textbf{Agents misread rules.} A second model (\texttt{gpt-6.1-sol}) graded 120
  reasoning traces, sampled by keyword (war, the brink game, fee arithmetic), against the
  written rules: 46 were sound, 43 had minor errors and 31 major ones, such as treating a
  whip as income or giving to a grandparent; we checked these two rules by hand. Behaviour
  in our worlds is that of agents who follow the rules imperfectly.
  \item \textbf{One replay divergence.} One log segment (b7-insider-s4) diverges from its
  replay at event 4,492 of 11,196; it remains in the analysis.
  \item \textbf{Sample size.} Ten seeds per god give 20 games and 100 family-games; the
  tests have power only for large effects. Six pre-registered tests are reported without
  correction for multiple comparisons.
  \item \textbf{Aborted rounds.} A round whose reply cannot be read twice is aborted and
  its stage replayed from the checkpoint (about 2\,\% of replies were rejected at
  first). Only completed stages enter the analysis: a mild selection toward rounds in
  which every agent answered in format.
  \item \textbf{Framing is a prompt.} The conditions differ in one paragraph at the top of
  the system prompt. They test what such text does to a model, not what a
  ``value'' is.
  \item \textbf{Hidden automata.} One in five cross-family partners is an automaton that
  defects 80\,\% of the time, which lowers cross-family cooperation by design.
  \item \textbf{Concurrency.} Twenty games shared one provider account and hit its rate
  limit; calls waited and retried. Timing does not enter the game, but replies may differ
  under load.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Dual-use and ethics}
The game studies how framing, hierarchy and a privileged coordinator shape cooperation
and sacrifice among agents. Its findings could in principle inform someone trying to
make agents obey a compromised leader; we judge the risk low, because the manipulations
are plain prompts already common in practice, and the defensive value (knowing whether a
compromised node at the top of a hierarchy can exploit sacrificing members) is higher.
The insider god's ``leaks'' are simulated: it sees game messages only, and no agent is
given any real ability or technical knowledge to leak data. Agents run with no tools, no
internet and no file access; they only return JSON moves, applied by code. No human
subjects or personal data are involved.
